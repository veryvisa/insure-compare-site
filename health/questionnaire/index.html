逐题说明（短问卷里的每一题）

下面每一题对应短问卷里的一题，问卷和 PDF 上的「详细说明」链接指到这里。几份问卷共用的题只写一次。

姓名（与证件一致，英文）

出现在：S1、S2、S3、S4、S6、S7

为什么问
保险公司要把您和您的资料对上号，名字必须和证件一致。
怎么答
照证件上的英文写，姓在后在前都可以，保持一致就行。孩子的表由家长代填，填孩子的名字。
常见误区
写小名、昵称或中文拼音简写。以后核对身份时对不上，会多出一轮补资料。
会影响什么
不影响费率；写错会拖慢后面的所有环节。

出生日期

出现在：S1、S2、S3、S4、S6、S7

为什么问
年龄是保费与体检要求的第一个因素；同一件健康事，不同年龄的看法也不同。
怎么答
写证件上的出生日期，格式 年-月-日。
常见误区
写成农历或写错月日。保险按证件上的年龄算，错了会影响保费与资格。
会影响什么
年龄越大，保费越高，通常要的检查也越多。

电子邮箱

出现在：S1、S2、S3、S4、S6、S7

为什么问
用来发回执和以后修改答案的链接，不会用来群发广告。
怎么答
写您平时看得到的邮箱。孩子的表写家长的邮箱。
常见误区
写了很少打开的邮箱，收不到回执和修改链接。
会影响什么
不影响投保结果。

证件所示性别

出现在：S1、S2、S4

男 ／ 女 ／ X

为什么问
部分化验指标和疾病风险，男女的看法不同。
怎么答
按证件上的性别选。
常见误区
把「想选的」当成「证件上的」。核保按证件。
会影响什么
通常影响很小；在个别疾病与妊娠相关的判断上才有区别。

您在加拿大的身份

出现在：S1、S2、S7

公民 ／ 永久居民 ／ 工签 ／ 学签 ／ 等待 PR 或省提名 ／ 访客或其他

为什么问
保险公司对永久居民、工签、学签、访客的可保范围和要求不同，也会影响能不能拿到医疗记录。
怎么答
选当前持有的身份。在等 PR 或省提名的，选「等待」那一项。
常见误区
把「已经申请」当成「已经拿到」。
会影响什么
身份会决定有没有资格买某类产品，以及要不要多交几份资料。新移民可再填 S7 附页。

身高与体重（请写单位）

出现在：S1、S2、S3、S4

为什么问
身高体重算出的体重指数（BMI）是定价的基础指标之一。
怎么答
写大概数字加单位就行，如 172 cm / 65 kg 或 5'8" / 150 lb。
常见误区
只写数字不写单位，或把磅当公斤写。
会影响什么
体重指数在常见范围内通常没有影响；明显偏高或偏低，可能影响费率，并可能要多问几题。

大概想要多少寿险保额（加元）

出现在：S1

小于 25 万 ／ 25–50 万 ／ 50–100 万 ／ 100–300 万 ／ 大于 300 万

为什么问
保额越大，通常要的体检和资料越多，所以先知道大概范围。
怎么答
选最接近的一档就行，不是承诺。约见时再按您的家庭情况定。
常见误区
为了避开体检而故意写小。保额以家庭实际需要为准。
会影响什么
保额越大，要的资料越多；落在免体检额度以内，通常可以少几项检查（各公司不同，待核验）。

过去 12 个月有没有用过尼古丁（香烟、电子烟、尼古丁贴片或口香糖）或大麻？

出现在：S1、S2、S3、S4、S6

否 ／ 是

为什么问
是否用尼古丁，是寿险与重疾险价格差别最大的一项；大麻也会影响分档。
怎么答
按过去 12 个月实际情况答。偶尔一两次也要如实写在最后的说明里。
常见误区
觉得「早就戒了」或「只是偶尔」就答否。核保时可能验尿或验血，查得到。
会影响什么
不用尼古丁的价格明显低于使用者。大麻看频率与方式，各公司分档不同。

近 5 年有没有住过院、做过手术，或做检查发现异常，或医生让您复查、做检查而还没做？

出现在：S1、S2

否 ／ 是

为什么问
近几年的住院、手术和异常检查，是核保师判断近期健康的主要线索；被建议做却没做的检查尤其会被追问。
怎么答
想得到就写，想不起细节就写「不确定」。体检时的小毛病、已治愈的小手术，也请在最后的说明里写一句。
常见误区
觉得「已经好了」或「不严重」就不提。保险公司可能从医疗信息库或医生报告里看到，后来才发现比一开始说明更麻烦。
会影响什么
一次已经恢复的小事，通常影响很小；还在查或还没做的检查，可能让核保延期，等结果出来再定。

目前有没有在长期吃处方药？（避孕药、维生素、偶尔的感冒药不算）

出现在：S1、S2

否 ／ 是

为什么问
长期吃的处方药，说明有一个需要长期管理的健康问题，核保师会通过药名倒推病情。
怎么答
只算医生开的、每天或长期吃的药。避孕药、维生素、偶尔的感冒药不算。有的话选「是」，并改填 S3 写药名。
常见误区
忘了写「为了预防」而吃的药（如降血脂药、抗凝药）。预防性用药同样算。
会影响什么
药名往往比病名更说明问题；稳定、剂量小的药通常影响有限，需要的话顾问会先匿名询价。

您的亲生父母、兄弟姐妹中，有没有人在 60 岁前得过心脏病、中风、癌症或糖尿病？

出现在：S1、S4

否 ／ 是

为什么问
家族史对重疾险尤其敏感；亲生父母、兄弟姐妹早发的心脏病、中风、癌症，说明风险可能更高。
怎么答
只算亲生的父母、兄弟姐妹，不算姻亲，也不算祖父母和堂表亲。「早发」按 60 岁前（孩子的表按 50 岁前）。不知道就选「是」旁边写「不确定」。
常见误区
把「年纪大了才得」也算进去，或者相反，把父母早逝的原因略掉。早发和晚发的区别很大。
会影响什么
家族史本身通常不会拒保；多位直系亲属早发同一种病时，可能加费或要更多检查。重疾险比寿险更看重。

体检与资料要求说明

核对日期 2026-11-19。各保险公司的额度和触发条件不同，以投保时保险公司的现行规定为准；查不到官方原文的数字一律写「待核验」。更多见体检与健康证明.

为什么有时要体检或医生报告

保险公司要求的资料，大致随三件事一起升高：年龄越大、保额越高、健康回答里的线索越多（诊断、用药、异常化验）。反过来，年轻、保额不高、回答干净时，常常只需要问卷加数据库查询。

常见的要求按轻重大致分六层（各公司叫法与触发条件不同，以投保时保险公司通知为准）：

① 只填问卷，保险公司核对行业信息库与处方用药记录；② 电话访谈，核保人员就问卷细节追问几分钟；③ 上门或到诊所体检：量身高体重、血压、脉搏，抽血、验尿（尿液可查尼古丁、大麻与药物；血液可查血糖、肝肾功能、血脂等，HIV 等项目需另行取得您的同意）；④ 心电图或运动心电图，多见于年龄较大、保额高或有心脏线索时；⑤ 主治医生报告（APS），有诊断、长期用药或异常结果时常见；⑥ 专项检查，如超声、前列腺特异抗原（PSA）、胸片、认知测试，以及保额很高时的收入与资产证明（财务核保）。

免体检的几条路与额度（已核事实与分歧）

加速核保：对符合条件的较健康申请人，不抽血验尿、不做体检，由问卷加数据模型直接决定。再保险公司 Munich Re 加拿大公布的统计：免体检（不取体液）的最高额度，在发单年龄 45 岁时，从 2017 年的 100 万加元升到 2023 年的 500 万；到 2024 年底稳定在 500 万、适用到发单年龄 50 岁。个人寿险保额中免体检的占比从 2017 年的 29% 升到 2023 年的 54%。仍有随机抽检（约 10% 的保单）和针对性抽检（约 14%）；全程自动出单约 25%。[MR1]

数字不一致的地方：一家经纪网站（2024 年 10 月）把保证承保写成常见 2.5 万至 5 万加元，简化核保「很多情况」最高约 25 万，加速核保最高约 50 万，免体检产品的年龄上限多在 50 至 60 岁。[LIC] 它比再保险公司统计保守得多；差别很可能来自不同公司、不同产品，也可能来自经纪网站的简化。结论：具体额度与年龄线必须以您申请那家公司的现行核保指引为准，**待核验**。

重疾险是否适用同样的免体检额度：公开资料没有查到统一口径，通常比寿险更谨慎，**待核验**。

2026 年可以留意的变化

电子健康记录。2026 年 2 月联邦政府提出 S-5 号法案（Connected Care for Canadians Act），目标是推动医疗信息系统互通、禁止阻碍数据共享；Munich Re 2026-06-25 的文章说，到 2026 年 4 月它已完成参议院委员会审议，尚未到众议院。法案不等于保险公司可随意调取病历：披露仍取决于您的同意、隐私法与各省医疗信息规则。如果将来落地，可能减少医生报告与体检的需要。目前加拿大可用的第三方医疗数据仍有限。[MR2]

数字医疗数据合作。Munich Re 加拿大与 ExamOne 在 2026 年公布合作，用数字化的医疗数据支持加拿大寿险核保；公告页上日期格式含糊，具体日期**待核验**。[MR3]

AI 核保助手。有报道说有保险公司向顾问推出 AI 核保助手，用来加快复杂案件的核保；具体公司与日期**待核验**。[ADV]

大麻。多家大公司自 2016 年起对「不用任何尼古丁的大麻使用者」可按非吸烟者定价[CAN]，但使用频率（有的经纪资料说每周多于 3 至 4 次，未经保险公司原文核实）、是否吸入，都可能改变分档。本次没有查到 2026 年全行业的新统一变化，各家以现行指引为准，问卷因此按频率与方式分开问。

减重药与减重手术。问卷里新增这一问（见第 5 节）。这是依据顾问实务，没有查到公开的统一核保口径，**待核验**。

您可以怎样提前准备

整理这几样，会让核保更快：长期用药的药名与剂量清单；最近一两次的血压、糖化血红蛋白（A1c）、胆固醇、肝肾功能化验单；有过癌症或心脏事件的，确诊日期与最后一次治疗结束日期，以及病理或出院报告；家庭医生的姓名与诊所。

如果被安排体检，常见的安排是：体检前 8 至 12 小时不吃东西（可以喝水）、前一天避免剧烈运动与饮酒、照常吃处方药、带好有照片的证件。以保险公司通知为准。

不确定自己的情况会不会影响投保时，可以先请顾问做匿名询价：不提供姓名，保险公司先给个大致的判断，通常不会以您的名字入库。这是常见做法，各公司是否接受以实际为准。

要记住的几件事

这份问卷是给您和顾问约见前做准备的，不是保险申请。正式申请时，保险公司会让您再回答并签名。答案不实，可能影响将来的理赔。

核保结果通常有五种：按标准价承保、加费、对某些情况除外、延期（等某个结果或等一段时间）、拒保。同一件事，不同公司的结论可能不同，所以不要因为一家的结果就放弃。

Two ways to fill it, and how each works

Fill in online (recommended)

  • Works on a phone or computer; about 3 to 8 minutes.
  • The pre-filled link already ticks the common No / None answers; change only what does not fit.
  • After submitting, an edit link arrives by email; use it any time to change answers, and your advisor sees the latest version.
  • Collected by Google Forms, so data may be stored outside Canada; if that matters to you, use the PDF.

Download the editable PDF

  • Two pages; click and type in the PDF itself, or print it and write by hand.
  • On a computer open it in Adobe Acrobat Reader or Mac Preview; on a phone use the Adobe Acrobat Reader app, since a browser's built-in viewer may not keep what you type.
  • Save the file (as a new copy) after filling, then send it back to your advisor; a screenshot is not enough.
  • The PDF travels only between you and your advisor, not through Google; send it by an email or messaging channel you trust.

After you submit: who sees it, how to change it, privacy

  • Who sees it: you and your advisor's account; no page of this website ever shows your answers.
  • How to change it: online, use the edit link in the confirmation email; for the PDF, save again and send it with a note saying which copy it replaces.
  • What it never asks for: SIN, bank accounts, ID numbers or card numbers. Tell us if any form seems to ask.
  • Retention: answers are used only to prepare the meeting, a clean-up reminder is raised once the retention period ends, and you can ask your advisor to delete them at any time.
  • Answer truthfully: this is not an application, but the questions on a real application must be answered accurately; wrong answers can void a policy or lead to a denied claim, so write not sure when unsure.

Full name as on ID

Used in: S1, S2, S3, S4, S6, S7

Why it is asked
The insurer needs to match you to your records, so the name must match your ID.
How to answer
Copy the English name from your ID. For a child's form, a parent fills in and writes the child's name.
Common slips
Using a nickname or an abbreviation. A mismatch later adds a round of paperwork.
What it may affect
It does not affect price; a wrong name only slows everything down.

Date of birth

Used in: S1, S2, S3, S4, S6, S7

Why it is asked
Age is the first factor in price and in what evidence is asked for; the same health fact is read differently at different ages.
How to answer
Use the date of birth on your ID, as year-month-day.
Common slips
Writing a lunar date or swapping month and day. Insurance uses the age on your ID.
What it may affect
A higher age usually means a higher price and more evidence.

Email

Used in: S1, S2, S3, S4, S6, S7

Why it is asked
It is used to send a receipt and a link for later edits, not for mass mailing.
How to answer
Write an address you actually read. For a child's form, use a parent's email.
Common slips
Using an inbox you rarely open, so the receipt and edit link are missed.
What it may affect
It does not affect the outcome.

Sex shown on ID

Used in: S1, S2, S4

Male / Female / X

Why it is asked
A few lab values and disease risks are read differently by sex.
How to answer
Pick the sex shown on your ID.
Common slips
Choosing what you prefer instead of what the ID shows. Underwriting follows the ID.
What it may affect
Usually a small effect; it matters only for a few conditions and pregnancy-related questions.

Your status in Canada

Used in: S1, S2, S7

Citizen / Permanent resident / Work permit / Study permit / PR / PNP pending / Visitor or other

Why it is asked
Insurers set different eligibility and requirements for residents, work or study permits, and visitors; it also affects how easily records can be obtained.
How to answer
Choose the status you hold now. If you are waiting for PR or a nomination, choose the pending option.
Common slips
Treating 'applied' as 'granted'.
What it may affect
Status decides whether some products are open to you and which extra papers are asked. New immigrants can add form S7.

Height and weight (with units)

Used in: S1, S2, S3, S4

Why it is asked
Height and weight give your body mass index (BMI), one of the basic pricing factors.
How to answer
A rough figure with units is fine, such as 172 cm / 65 kg or 5'8" / 150 lb.
Common slips
Writing a number without units, or pounds as kilograms.
What it may affect
A BMI in the usual range generally has no effect; a clearly high or low figure may affect price and bring extra questions.

What are you looking at

Used in: S1

Term life / Permanent or participating life / Not sure

Why it is asked
Term and permanent are underwritten to the same health standard, but amount and term change which tests are asked for.
How to answer
If unsure, choose 'Not sure'; the advisor compares them at the meeting.
Common slips
Assuming 'cheap' means 'term only'. The two serve different purposes.
What it may affect
It does not change the health view; it shapes the amount and term discussed next.

Roughly how much life cover (CAD)

Used in: S1

< 250k / 250–500k / 500k–1M / 1–3M / > 3M

Why it is asked
The larger the amount, the more exams and papers are usually asked for, so we first need a rough range.
How to answer

In the past 12 months, any nicotine (cigarettes, vape, patch, gum) or cannabis?

Used in: S1, S2, S3, S4, S6

No / Yes

Why it is asked
Nicotine use is the largest single price factor in life and critical illness cover; cannabis can also change the class.
How to answer
Answer for the past 12 months. Even occasional use should be mentioned on the last page.
Common slips
Answering No because you 'quit long ago' or 'only sometimes'. Urine or blood tests can detect use.
What it may affect
Non-users pay clearly less than users. For cannabis, insurers look at frequency and method and differ in their classes.

In the past 5 years: a hospital stay, surgery, an abnormal test, or a test a doctor asked for that is still not done?

Used in: S1, S2

No / Yes

Why it is asked
Recent hospital stays, surgery and abnormal tests are the main clues to recent health; a test that was advised but not done is asked about most.
How to answer
Answer what you recall; write 'not sure' for details. Even a minor, resolved surgery deserves one line on the last page.
Common slips
Leaving it out because it is 'healed' or 'minor'. The insurer may see it in medical information databases or in a doctor's report, and discovering it later is harder than saying so first.
What it may affect
A small resolved event usually has little effect; a pending or undone test can postpone the decision until the result is in.

Do you take prescription medicine regularly? (Birth control, vitamins and occasional cold medicine do not count.)

Used in: S1, S2

No / Yes

Why it is asked
A prescription taken regularly points to a health issue that needs ongoing management; underwriters read the condition from the drug name.
How to answer
Count only prescribed medicine taken daily or long term. Birth control, vitamins and occasional cold medicine do not count. If yes, choose Yes and also fill in S3 with the drug names.
Common slips
Forgetting medicine taken 'to prevent' something, such as a cholesterol or blood-thinning drug. Preventive medicine counts too.
What it may affect
The drug name often says more than the diagnosis; a stable, low dose usually has limited impact, and the advisor can ask an insurer anonymously first.

Did a parent or sibling have heart disease, stroke, cancer or diabetes before age 60?

Used in: S1, S4

No / Yes

Why it is asked
Family history weighs most for critical illness cover; early heart disease, stroke or cancer in parents or siblings can signal higher risk.
How to answer
Count only blood parents and siblings, not in-laws, grandparents or cousins. 'Early' means before 60 (before 50 on the child's form). If you do not know, answer Yes and write 'not sure'.
Common slips
Counting illness that came late in life, or leaving out the cause of a parent's early death. Early versus late makes a big difference.
What it may affect
Family history alone usually does not lead to a decline; several close relatives with the same early disease may mean a surcharge or more tests. It matters more for critical illness than for life.

High-risk work or hobbies (diving, skydiving, climbing, racing, flying, underground or heights), or living abroad 6+ months in the next 12?

Used in: S1

No / Yes

Why it is asked
High-risk work or hobbies and long stays abroad affect eligibility and claim terms.
How to answer
Answer for the past and next 12 months. One taster dive is not a 'hobby', but write a line about it.
Common slips
Skipping it because you do it 'once a year'. A dispute after a claim is worse for you.
What it may affect
It may mean a surcharge, an exclusion for that activity, or more questions; long stays abroad can affect eligibility.

When each was first diagnosed (e.g. hypertension 2019-05; separate with semicolons)

Used in: S3

Why it is asked
How long ago and at what age it was found is key to judging how serious the same condition is.
How to answer
One year-month per condition, like 'hypertension 2019-05', separated by semicolons. If unsure write 'about 2019'.
Common slips
Writing 'many years ago'. The underwriter needs a rough year.
What it may affect
A long, stable history often helps; a recent diagnosis may mean waiting before a decision.

Overall, how well controlled are they now

Used in: S3

Stable, no change in a year / Mostly stable, medication adjusted / Unstable or worsening / Not sure

Why it is asked
For the same condition, how steady it is often matters more than its name.
How to answer
Choose based on the latest view from you and your doctor. If unsure choose 'Not sure' rather than guess for the doctor.
Common slips
Reading 'normal on medicine' as 'no condition'. Needing medicine is itself evidence of control.
What it may affect
A stable, unchanged condition usually gets a better outcome; an unstable or worsening one may be postponed until it settles.

Exams and records: what to expect

Checked 2026-11-19. Insurers differ in limits and triggers; the insurer's current rules at application govern, and any figure without an official original is marked "to be verified". See also Medical exams and evidence.

Why an exam or doctor's report may be asked

What an insurer asks for rises with three things together: older age, a larger amount, and more clues in your health answers (a diagnosis, a medicine, an unusual result). The reverse also holds: when you are young, the amount is modest and the answers are clean, a questionnaire plus database checks is often enough.

Requests fall roughly into six layers (names and triggers differ by insurer): 1 a questionnaire plus checks against industry and prescription databases; 2 a tele-interview; 3 a paramedical visit with height, weight, blood pressure, pulse, blood and urine; 4 an ECG or stress ECG; 5 an attending physician's statement; 6 special tests such as ultrasound or PSA, and, for very large amounts, financial underwriting.

No-exam routes and amounts (checked facts and disagreement)

Accelerated underwriting: for suitable healthy applicants, no blood or urine and no exam; a questionnaire plus data models decide. Munich Re Canada reports that the no-exam ceiling at issue age 45 rose from CAD 1 million in 2017 to 5 million in 2023, and by the end of 2024 held at 5 million up to issue age 50. No-exam cases rose from 29% of individual life amounts in 2017 to 54% in 2023. Random checks (about 10% of policies) and targeted checks (about 14%) remain; about 25% are fully automatic. [MR1]

Where the numbers disagree: one broker site (October 2024) gives guaranteed issue at CAD 25,000 to 50,000, simplified at about 250,000, accelerated up to about 500,000, with age limits mostly 50 to 60. [LIC] That is far more conservative than the reinsurer's data, perhaps because of different insurers and products. Conclusion: amounts and age lines must come from the current guide of the insurer you apply to. To be verified.

Whether critical illness cover shares the same no-exam amounts: no unified public figure was found; it is usually more cautious than life. To be verified.

What changed or is changing in 2026

Electronic health records: Bill S-5 (Connected Care for Canadians Act) was tabled in February 2026; Munich Re's 25 June 2026 article says it had cleared the Senate committee by April 2026 and not yet reached the House. A bill is not a licence for insurers to pull records: disclosure still depends on your consent, privacy law and provincial rules. [MR2]

Digital medical data: Munich Re Canada and ExamOne announced a partnership in 2026 on digital medical data for Canadian life underwriting; the exact date is unclear. To be verified. [MR3]

AI underwriting assistants: reports say some insurers launched assistants for advisors; the insurers and dates are to be verified. [ADV]

Cannabis: since 2016 some large insurers can treat cannabis users with no nicotine as non-smokers [CAN], but frequency and inhalation can still change the class; no new 2026 industry-wide rule was found, so the questionnaire asks frequency and method separately.

Weight-loss drugs and surgery: a new question was added. It follows advisor practice with no unified public rule found. To be verified.

How to prepare

Gather: a list of regular medicines with doses; the last one or two blood-pressure, A1c, cholesterol and liver-kidney results; for cancer or heart events, the diagnosis date, the date the last treatment ended and the pathology or discharge report; your family doctor's name and clinic.

If an exam is booked, the usual instructions are to fast 8 to 12 hours (water is fine), avoid hard exercise and alcohol the day before, take prescribed medicine as usual and bring photo ID. Follow the insurer's notice.

If you are unsure whether something affects cover, the advisor can ask an insurer anonymously first: no name is given and the insurer gives a general view. This is common practice; whether an insurer accepts it varies.

Things to remember

These forms prepare you and your advisor for the meeting; they are not an application. At application the insurer asks again and you sign. Untrue answers can affect a later claim.

Outcomes usually fall into five kinds: standard, rated (surcharge), exclusion, postponed, declined. Insurers can differ on the same fact, so one result is not a reason to give up.
